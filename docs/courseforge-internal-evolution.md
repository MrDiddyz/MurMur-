## Operating Principles

CourseForge v1.0 establishes the internal foundation for an adaptive course-building system that continuously improves learning products through measured outcomes, review loops, and reusable knowledge structures.

### Self-evolutionary learning loop

The learning loop turns course performance signals into structured improvements. Each iteration should observe learner outcomes, identify friction, propose curriculum changes, review those changes against the 12-point performance standard, and preserve approved learning in the permanent knowledge structure.

### 12-point performance review

Every course module, lesson, and offer should be evaluated against a consistent review rubric before promotion. The review should prioritize measurable learner progress, clarity of the promised transformation, curriculum coherence, feedback quality, operational feasibility, and monetization fit.

### Permanent knowledge structure

CourseForge knowledge should be stored as reusable strategic memory: accepted patterns, rejected patterns, curriculum decisions, audience insights, offer mechanics, review outcomes, and evidence from learner behavior.

### Forbidden patterns

Forbidden patterns are anti-patterns that CourseForge should avoid by default, including content-first module design, vague learner outcomes, unvalidated monetization assumptions, overbroad lesson scopes, unreviewed AI-generated curriculum, and decisions that cannot be traced to evidence.

### Outcome-first course architecture

Course architecture starts with the learner transformation, then maps backward to assessments, milestones, lessons, exercises, and supporting content. Content exists to produce the outcome; it is not the product by itself.

### AI-native curriculum strategy

AI-native curriculum design treats AI as part of the course operating system: it can help generate variants, diagnose learner gaps, adapt practice, simulate feedback, and convert performance data into next-step improvements.

### Monetization-aware module design

Each module should clarify how it supports product value, upgrade paths, retention, proof of progress, buyer confidence, or measurable business outcomes without weakening the learner experience.
